01 Researcher · Political Economy of the Environment

Charlotte
Sophia Bez

I study the political economy of the environment — the obstacles to climate action, the geography of large-scale industries, and the distributive politics of the low-carbon transition.

I am a postdoctoral fellow at the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research (PIK), in the Climate Economics and Policy Department, and an incoming Walter Benjamin Scholar at the Chair of Computational Social Science at the European University Institute in Florence. Until June 2025 I was a Fulbright Schumann scholar at the Tishman Environment and Design Center at The New School in New York, where I remain a visiting researcher.

My work combines causal econometrics with text-as-data methods to study climate obstruction, just transition narratives, environmental inequality, and the geography of energy-intensive industries, including AI data centres. I co-lead the CSSN project Peddling false solutions: corporate carbon management and climate delay strategies with Thomas Klug at Brown University.
